# How AI 500 is selected

AI 500 asks who has a demonstrable influence on how artificial intelligence is researched, built, distributed, used, and governed. The unit is a person, not an organization. The target is 500 distinct living people with a continuing connection to the field.

## What counts as influence

We look for evidence of contributions that other people use or respond to: research methods and discoveries; adopted software and models; consequential product or infrastructure decisions; public policy and institutional leadership; independent scrutiny; and practical advances in science, health, education, and access.

Each inclusion rationale must identify that contribution. A prestigious title, conference appearance, social following, or entry on someone else's list is a lead for investigation. It is not sufficient evidence by itself. Influential criticism and opposition count as influence. Inclusion is not endorsement.

The directory is grouped into eight areas. Categories describe a principal route of influence and overlap in practice. People appear once, with additional interests represented by tags. Within categories, names are alphabetical. There is no fabricated influence score or ordinal ranking.

## Evidence and time

Each profile links to at least two substantive sources, with at least one primary source such as an institutional biography, original paper, official announcement, or project documentation. Source notes explain what each supports. Contribution claims and editorial rationales carry source references; present roles require primary evidence.

The edition date is a research cutoff, not a promise of live updates. A source's publication date, the date a role is established, and the date we checked the page are separate fields. Unknown publication dates stay unknown. A recently accessed biography may still be stale. Conflicts and unresolved limitations must be visible, and unsupported candidates remain outside the completed collection.

Recent activity is preferred. Older foundational contributions can support inclusion when there is evidence of continuing relevance or participation. Historical achievements alone do not establish that someone is influential today.

## Coverage and limitations

Research spans regions and includes universities, companies, governments, nonprofits, and independent communities. Public documentation is uneven. English-language sources, prominent institutions, and well-funded organizations are easier to discover; this can bias selection. The directory makes no claim to statistically representative geographic or demographic coverage. It does not infer personal demographic attributes from names or photographs.

All entries combine AI-assisted research with completed human editorial review. Structural checks can count identities, resolve references, and detect inconsistent dates. They cannot prove that a claim is true, that two publishers are independent, or that these are objectively the world's 500 most influential people.

## Corrections

Corrections, challenges to inclusion, and well-supported omissions are welcome through [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). A correction should identify the person, disputed claim, and relevant source. Changes of employer or title should include dated evidence when possible.
